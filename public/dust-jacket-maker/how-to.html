How to make a dust jacket

From a tape measure to a jacket on the book, in six steps.

1. Measure the book

Use a ruler, not a sewing tape, and measure the boards (the hard covers), not the pages:

  • Book height: top edge of the board to the bottom edge.
  • Front cover width: from where the board meets the spine to the board's outer edge.
  • Spine width: straight across the spine with the book closed, from the outside of the front board to the outside of the back board. A strip of paper wrapped across the spine and marked with a pencil is easier than a ruler on a round spine.
  • Flap width: your choice. About half the cover width is usual; 3 to 3.5 inches (75 to 90 mm) on a novel.

2. Enter the measurements

On the tool page, choose inches or millimetres and type the four numbers. Inches can be typed as fractions too, like 9 1/2. The wrap allowance (1/8 inch or 3 mm by default) is added to the height and to each cover so the paper reaches round the board edges. Add a little more for very thick boards.

3. Add your artwork

  • Front cover: a JPG, PNG or WebP image. A sample cover is shown until you choose your own.
  • Back cover and spine: an image, or a flat colour. The spine can have a line of text, which reads from top to bottom.
  • Flaps: a flat colour.
  • Fill crops an image to cover the whole panel; Fit shows all of it with the panel colour round it.

For a sharp print, aim for images of at least 150, and ideally 300, pixels per inch at the printed size. The tool shows the figure under each image and warns when it is low.

4. Choose bleed

Tick Add bleed if a print shop will cut the jacket, or if your design runs to the edge. The artwork then runs a little past the trim (1/8 inch or 3 mm by default) and is cut off, so there is no white edge.

5. Print

At a print shop: send the full-size PDF. It is one page at the exact jacket size, with trim marks at the corners, dashed fold marks at each panel edge and the trim and bleed sizes set in the file. Ask for it at 100%, on paper around 100 to 150 gsm (70 to 100 lb text). Gloss or matte paper both work.

At home: choose Letter or A4 and download the tiled PDF. The tool says how many sheets it takes. Print at actual size (100%), not fit to page. Each sheet shares a half inch strip with its neighbours. Lay the sheets out in order, cut each sheet along the line between its two cut marks, lay it over the sheet before so the cut edge sits on that sheet's match marks, and tape along the back. Join each row from left to right, then join the rows.

6. Trim and fold

Cut along the lines between the trim marks at the corners. Lay the jacket face down, put the closed book on it with the spine between the two middle fold marks, and crease along the spine edges. Wrap the covers, close the book to check the fit, then fold the flaps in at the outer fold marks and tuck them inside the covers.

If it does not fit

Too tight round the spine? Measure the spine again with the book closed and add a millimetre or two. Flaps slipping out? Make them wider. Jacket too short top to bottom? Increase the wrap allowance.
